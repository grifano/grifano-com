TachoBook — Shift Logging & Rest-Time App for EU Drivers

An offline-first mobile UX/UI case study exploring manual shift logging and break tracking for commercial truck drivers navigating complex EU driving-time regulations.

Problem & Context

Strict EU tachograph driving-time rules carry heavy fines for mistakes. Commercial drivers need an immediate, error-proof tool usable during short cabin breaks.

My Role

UX/UI Designer — Information architecture, user flows, low-to-high-fidelity wireframes, iOS design system tokens, and interactive Figma prototyping.

Key Design Decision

Engineered high-contrast touch targets and glanceable color-coded status rings to guarantee legibility and error prevention under direct sun or night cabin light.

Domain Insight

Informed by firsthand commercial trucking experience in Europe, grounding ergonomics and interaction speed in authentic driver workflows.

02 / About Me

Design Approach & Background

My commercial background is grounded in client projects, combining human-centered interface design with hands-on Webflow implementation. I work across the design process: user flows, wireframes, high-fidelity prototypes, responsive layouts, and reusable component libraries.

I am focused on joining a product team where I can contribute to long-term digital products, with a particular passion for web and mobile/iOS experiences.

Before transitioning into design and tech, my years working as a commercial driver across Europe provided me with practical, firsthand understanding of real-world operational ergonomics — the direct foundation behind my TachoBook case study.

Because I write HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, I design with a realistic appreciation for engineering feasibility, layout mechanics, and web performance.
